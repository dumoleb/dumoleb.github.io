Setting up Yuki

From a fresh install to watching, in about five minutes. Everything here is done with the remote control - there is no phone app, no pairing step and no account to create.

You need your own source. Yuki ships with no channels and no films. It plays what you point it at: an IPTV playlist you subscribe to, an Xtream Codes portal, or your own Jellyfin server. If you do not have one of those yet, nothing below will show any content - that is expected, not a fault.

1. First run: choose your language

The first screen offers twelve languages - English, Hebrew, Arabic, Russian, Spanish, French, German, Portuguese, Italian, Greek, Turkish and Polish. Pick one and press the centre button. Right-to-left languages mirror the whole interface, not just the text.

You can change this later in Settings, and nothing you set up is lost when you do.

2. Choose how your provider gives you access

The next screen asks a single question, and the answer decides everything that follows. Three kinds of source are supported:

Choose thisWhen your provider gave you
M3U / M3U8 playlist A single long web link, usually ending in .m3u or .m3u8, sometimes called a "playlist URL" or "line"
Xtream Codes Three separate things: a server address, a username and a password
Jellyfin server Your own media server at home - an address and your Jellyfin login

If you were given both a playlist link and a username and password, prefer Xtream Codes. It gives Yuki more to work with: a proper programme guide, films and series as separate libraries, and catch-up where your provider offers it. A plain playlist link can only describe a list of channels.

Adding an M3U playlist

Choose M3U / M3U8 playlist, then type or paste the link. On a television the on-screen keyboard is the slow part - if the link is long, it is worth pasting it from a keyboard app or a USB keyboard if you have one attached.

Give the source a name you will recognise later ("Living room provider" is more useful than "Playlist 1"), and press Add. Yuki downloads the list and tells you how many channels it found.

Adding an Xtream Codes portal

Choose Xtream Codes and fill in the three fields exactly as your provider sent them:

  • Server address - often looks like http://example.com:8080. Include the port number if there is one, and do not add anything after it.
  • Username - case-sensitive.
  • Password - also case-sensitive.

A common cause of "cannot connect" here is a trailing slash or a copied space at the end of a field. If it fails, retype the server address by hand before assuming the credentials are wrong.

Adding a Jellyfin server

Choose Jellyfin server, enter your server's address and sign in with your Jellyfin username and password. If your server runs on your home network, use its local address; if you reach it from outside, use the address you normally use in a browser.

3. Wait for the first load

The first load is the slowest one. Yuki downloads your channel list and, where your provider supplies it, the programme guide - which can be several days of listings for thousands of channels. After that it refreshes quietly in the background and you should not see this wait again.

5. While watching

Press the centre button during playback to bring up the overlay without stopping what you are watching. From there you can reach the channel list, the schedule for the channel you are on, and the audio and subtitle tracks. Press Back to dismiss it.

Two shortcuts are worth learning, because they skip the overlay entirely:

Press and holdOpens
DownThe channel list, over what you are watching
UpThe schedule for the current channel

If a stream refuses to play, Yuki retries it with a second playback engine before giving up - many awkward streams that fail elsewhere simply work here, without you doing anything.

6. Watching something that has already aired

Where your provider supports catch-up, you reach it through the Guide rather than through a menu of its own: move back through the programme grid to a show that has already finished and open it. If that particular programme is not available to replay, Yuki says so rather than failing silently.

Catch-up depends entirely on your provider. Two channels on the same subscription can differ, and how far back it reaches is their decision, not the app's.

7. Worth setting up once

Favourites

Mark the channels you actually watch. A list of eight favourites is far more useful on a sofa than a list of eight hundred channels, and it survives a playlist refresh.

Parental controls

Settings has a PIN, per-channel locks, and scheduled locks and screen-time limits. The PIN protects the settings themselves, so a child cannot simply switch the locks off. It is stored on your television and is not recoverable by me - write it down.

Accessibility

Text size, high-visibility focus and how long menus stay on screen are all adjustable, and they apply across the whole app rather than to one screen. If you sit further than three metres from the television, the larger text steps are worth a try.

Profiles

Each person in the house can have their own profile, with their own favourites, viewing history and resume positions. Changing your text size or your row order does not change anyone else's. Connected sources and the parental PIN belong to the television and are shared.

If something does not work

Start with the troubleshooting page, which explains what each of Yuki's error messages means. If nothing there fits, the support page lists the few details that turn a week of guessing into a same-day fix. The most common causes are a mistyped server address, an expired subscription, and a provider that is simply down - none of which the app can tell apart from the outside without your help.

Never send anyone your playlist URL, portal password or server login - including me. I have no access to your provider and cannot use them to help you, and they are all somebody would need to use your subscription.
